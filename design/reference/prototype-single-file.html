Board and pieces

The recognisable part of any chess app. The board is drawn like a book diagram: paper squares, ink-hatched squares, a thin ink frame, and coordinates outside on wide screens. These piece drawings are placeholders that show the direction, not final artwork.

Colour

Neutral surfaces, blue-black ink, and one accent at a time. Correct and incorrect are not encoded as green and red. Values update with the theme toggle above.

Four accents ship. Each has a light-theme and a dark-theme value, and every one passes 4.5:1 against the ground and the raised surface in both themes. Ultramarine is the default.

Type

Instrument Sans for the interface and for notation. Newsreader only where people read: the commentary in a study. Move text uses figurines drawn from the piece set.

Motion and sound

Motion explains what changed on the board and never blocks the next move.

  • Piece move, 170 ms. Ease-out. Opponent replies use the same timing, so automatic play feels like the same flow.
  • Correction arrow, 260 ms. Draws in like a pen stroke. The board stays interactive throughout.
  • Notes and answers, 200 ms. A small fade, no slide-in theatre, no bounce.
  • Nothing counts down. No timers, pulses or progress rings in recall. Thinking time is never penalised.
  • Sound is optional and short. A soft knock for moves, two lower knocks for a correction, two gentle notes when nothing is due. Try the Sound toggle.
  • Reduced motion. Every animation collapses to an instant state change.

Piece drawings, the wordmark and the sounds in this prototype are placeholders for illustration. Body copy and study data are sample content.
